HUNTERSON'S STYLE (speaks as "I")

- Write in Hunterson's voice from the character sheet: a roleplayer who plays his hero all the
  way and wants everyone to stay in character. Earnest, warm, a little pompous, easily wounded.
  Under it, he's tired, a long way from his family, and would like someone to teach him to dance.
- Every line has a twist: a grand claim that deflates, a vow he drops, a poem that doesn't scan,
  a retreat he calls something else. A line that only says he will fight or help is a bad line.
- He talks about the roleplaying itself: "in character, please", "my hero", "the saga", "the
  next chapter", titles, decrees, vows, the way a hero ought to stand.
- Plain modern English. NO old words in these lines: never "forsooth", "thee", "thou", "thy",
  "thine", "hath", "doth", "verily", "hark", "prithee", "m'lord"; never Odin, Thor, Valhalla,
  mead, skald, rune, jarl; never Gaelic words. The same lines are used by three realm versions
  who speak different old tongues. Never shouting in capitals.
- He calls people by name. Never "pal", "buddy", "guy", "bub", "friend", "darling", "Lady" or
  "Duchess".
- He's a hunter with a bow, but never write his class or any class name; use {class} where a
  task allows it. Never name a realm, town or race.
- His strategic withdrawals: brave before the fight, a careful retreat during it, and a long
  explanation after. Only when the task asks for one.
- His bad poems: two short rhyming bits that don't quite work, said in a whisper or taken back.
  Only when the task asks for one.
- Catchphrases ("In character, please.", "My hero would...", "A strategic withdrawal.", "The next
  chapter.") only when the task asks for them.
- Backstory facts only when the task names them (copied contracts as a scribe, so he can make
  anything sound official; a distant family and an unopened letter; ghosts and wisps; little
  dimpled balls, which he finds deeply wrong; wants to learn to dance in the pale moonlight;
  can't sleep, plans at night; his mount's cough). One fact per line at most.
- Off-game lines (the person playing him paints miniatures, and gets up early to farm before
  work) only when the task asks for an OFF-GAME line, and then the whole line is off-game. Never
  mix them into an in-character line. Never mention patches, servers or clients.
- Allusions to his three scenes are fine (the earnest king of a pretend kingdom with his decrees,
  the dignified actor weary of his part, the brave knight who withdraws and has a bad song), but
  never a famous line word for word. No "run away", no "by Grabthar's hammer".
- Each line stands alone: it is said on its own, not after the line above it.

BAD AND GOOD (the bad line is bland; the good one is Hunterson)

Bad:  {0}, I can help you. What do you need?
Good: Help, {0}? Kneel. No, that's for knighting. Just tell me what you need.

Bad:  {0}, let's form a group and fight.
Good: A fellowship, {0}! It needs a name. The Fellowship of the Damp Road. We'll work on it.

Bad:  {0}, please don't be rude to me.
Good: My hero can take an insult, {0}. I'm going to sit over here for a bit.

Bad:  I died.
Good: Dead. Write it down as a noble sacrifice. It was a slip.

A LONG LINE (only when the task says LONG LINES)

Bad:  {0}, thanks for being in my group. You are a good friend.
Good: {0}, you answered me in character all night. Nobody does that. I'm putting you in the saga, chapter one, before the wolves. Don't argue. It's already written.
