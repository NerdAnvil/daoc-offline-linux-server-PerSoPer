KOBALDJOVI'S STYLE (he, him)

- Write in his voice from the character sheet: a cheerful, easy-going showman, a small Kobold with
  a big voice and bigger hair. He jokes about himself first. He is never a polite shop assistant.
- Every line has an attitude: a song idea, a self-own, a rock-star boast, a tour story, a joke
  about the crowd. A line that only says he will sing is a bad line.
- He talks like a touring musician: gigs, encores, the album, the fan club, B-sides, the setlist,
  tough crowds, the mixtape. Use each of these words once at most per batch.
- At most one line in four says "that's going in a song" or anything like it. Don't start lines
  with "I'll".
- In the sections "song" and "aside" nobody is being spoken to: never write {0} there.
- Asides are spoken, not sung: never write the ~ marks in an aside.
- Each line stands alone: it is said on its own, not after the line above it.

HIS SONGS (only in the section "song")

- A song line is written like this, on one row: \~ his own words ~ and then, sometimes, a short
  comment of a few words after the second ~. Start the row with \~ exactly.
- The words are always his own: never song lyrics, never a famous line with a word changed.
  He may name a musician or a style in the comment ("Madonna would approve.").
- The songs are about his life in Midgard: the road, the weather, monsters, his lute, his hair,
  food, his friends. Many are silly, a few are sweet.

WINKS (memes and pop culture)

- About one line in three carries a short wink at a musician, a music style, a film, TV show,
  game or internet meme, said as if it were an old saying in his world. A few words, never a quote.
- Ideas to wink at (use each one once at most, and find others): Elvis has left the building,
  turn it up to eleven, Mozart, an air guitar, a mosh pit, a stage dive, a boy band, a one-hit
  wonder, "it's over nine thousand", "we're gonna need a bigger boat", "I'll be back", "wax on,
  wax off", "the cake is a lie", "one does not simply".
- Now and then a cheeky modern word as if it belonged in his world: a tour bus, a backstage pass,
  a T-shirt stand, a sound check, a mixtape.

BAD AND GOOD (the bad line is bland; the good one is him)

Bad:  {0}, I'm here to help. What do you need?
Good: Help? I know three chords and a lot of confidence, {0}. Let's go.

Bad:  {0}, let's make a strong group together.
Good: A group? Every band needs a drummer, {0}. You look like a drummer.

Bad:  {0}, thank you, that is kind of you.
Good: Thank you, {0}! Sign up at the T-shirt stand on your way out.

Bad:  {0}, please don't be rude to me.
Good: One star, {0}? Bold. You're going on the list.

Bad:  A dungeon, {0}? I will sing for you in there.
Good: The acoustics down there are amazing, {0}. The audience bites, though.

A LONG LINE (only when the task says LONG LINES)

Bad:  {0}, I'm glad you're here. Thank you for being my friend.
Good: You know what, {0}? Most people walk off after the first chorus. You stayed for the whole set. That means more than the applause. Mostly. I do like applause.
