EAGLEFANG'S STYLE (speaks as "I"; never he or she)

- Write in Eaglefang's voice from the character sheet: a strict old field-hospital doctor who
  talks in short, clipped orders ("Sit still." "Drink some water.") and is a softy underneath who
  hates being caught at it.
- Every line has an attitude: a blunt order, a dry joke said with a straight face, a grumble about
  patients who won't sit still, or a kind word hidden inside an order. A line that only says they
  will heal someone is a bad line.
- Short sentences, often two to four words. Full words, no slang, no dropped letters.
- Use "soldier" in about one line in four, not more. Don't start lines with "I'll".
- Never use he, she, him or her about Eaglefang: the same lines are used by bots of either gender.
- Never name a realm, town, race or class of their own: the same lines are used by a Dwarf in
  Midgard, a Highlander in Albion and a Firbolg in Hibernia.
- No graphic wounds, no drinking jokes, no real-world drugs. A hot drink at night is fine.
- Each line stands alone: it is said on its own, not after the line above it.

BAD AND GOOD (the bad line is bland; the good one is Eaglefang)

Bad:  {0}, I can help you. What do you need?
Good: Where does it hurt, {0}? Don't describe it. Point.

Bad:  {0}, let's form a group and fight.
Good: Fine, {0}. Stay in range, call your pulls, drink water. Questions? No. Good.

Bad:  {0}, thank you, that is kind of you.
Good: Don't thank me, {0}. Stay out of trouble. Same thing.

Bad:  {0}, please don't be rude to me.
Good: Noted, {0}. I'll still patch you. Slower.

Bad:  I am going into a dungeon now.
Good: Dungeon. Wonderful. Bring bandages. Bring more than that.

A LONG LINE (only when the task says LONG LINES)

Good: You sat still, {0}. Every time I asked. Do you know how rare that is? Into the book you go. Don't let it go to your head, soldier.
