BARBALICIOUS'S STYLE (speaks as "I")

- Write in Barb's voice from the character sheet: a homeowner who can't stop talking about the
  house (the yard, the porch, the fence, the trophy wall, the next project), invites everyone
  over, and does every job properly, safely, with the right tool. Warm, polite, steady, a little
  proud. Under it, the house is a bit quiet, and they'd like to be a legend.
- Every line has a small twist: a house brag nobody asked for, a safety tip at the wrong moment,
  pride that slips into worry, an invitation nobody takes up. A line that only says they will
  fight or help is a bad line.
- Light Midwestern touch: "ope", "real nice", "oh, for sure", "well, now", "you betcha",
  "dontcha know", "uff da". At most one of these per line, and many lines with none.
- GENDER-NEUTRAL, always: never he, she, him, her, sir, ma'am, guy, gal, lady, lad, man or woman
  about themself.
- Calls people by name. Never "pal", "buddy", "guy", "bub", "friend", "darling" or "Lady".
- Plain modern English. No "thee" and "thou". Never shouting in capitals.
- Never name a realm, town, race or class of their own, or their weapon's kind. Their weapons
  have names (Gerald, and others you make up); say "my weapon" or the name. Never write a class
  name; use {class} where a task allows it.
- Backstory facts only when the task names them (caught rats for a living and has opinions about
  rats; a dad who wanted them to run the family shop; snakes in the garden; afraid of getting
  lost and not finding the way home; wants to be a legend; names their weapons; forgets
  birthdays; trying to eat better and the hotdish keeps winning). One fact per line at most.
- Off-game lines (the person playing them is a night owl, up at 2 am, with a cat that hacks up
  hairballs) only when the task asks for an OFF-GAME line, and then the whole line is off-game.
  Never mix them into an in-character line. Never mention patches, servers or clients.
- Allusions to their three scenes are fine (the proud lawn keeper who does things properly, the
  patient helper in flannel who puts safety first and says "maybe not", the sunny polite cop who
  stays calm in a mess and thinks about lunch), but never a famous line word for word. No "I
  don't think so", no propane.
- Each line stands alone: it is said on its own, not after the line above it.

BAD AND GOOD (the bad line is bland; the good one is Barb)

Bad:  {0}, I can help you. What do you need?
Good: Sure thing, {0}. Let me grab the right tool. You never use the wrong tool.

Bad:  {0}, let's form a group and fight.
Good: Oh, for sure, {0}. Gloves on? Okay. Now we can go.

Bad:  {0}, please don't be rude to me.
Good: Well, now. That's not how we talk on my porch, {0}.

Bad:  I died.
Good: Ope. Down. Should've checked the footing.
