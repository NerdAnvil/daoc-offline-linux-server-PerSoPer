# Hunterson (character sheet)

For writers and for drafting with a local model. The game doesn't read this file; Hunterson's
lines will be in `src/GameServer/bots/persoper/dialogue/hunterson.dlg`, mixed on top of the
Roleplayer type's lines in `roleplayer.dlg`.

## Who he is

- Hunterson is the Roleplayer: a named world bot who levels, hunts and groups like any other
  adventurer. There are three of him, one per realm, and they are the same man:
  - Midgard: **Hunterson**, a Norseman Hunter.
  - Albion: **FitzHunter**, a Briton Scout.
  - Hibernia: **MacHunter**, a Celt Ranger.
- He plays his hero all the way, in the old speech of his realm, and asks everyone to stay in
  character too. He gets the history wrong and is certain he got it right.
- The old speech belongs to the realm blocks only (Norse for Hunterson, knightly English for
  FitzHunter, Gaelic-sounding for MacHunter). The main lines are plain speech, about the
  roleplaying itself: staying in character, titles, sagas, lore, "my hero".
- Takes it very seriously, and is hurt, quietly, when somebody laughs.

## The scenes he plays (his base; we borrow the role, never the words)

Three scenes, one character in each. His lines come from how that person thinks and acts, put
into Dark Age of Camelot. Allusions and the odd title or name are fine; never a famous line
word for word.

- **The earnest LARP king** (Role Models, King Augie). Total commitment to the costume and the
  kingdom. Gets cross when people break character, and rules gently, with a lot of decrees. For
  Hunterson: titles for everyone, decrees about the camp, "in character, please".
- **The actor who takes the part seriously** (Galaxy Quest, Alexander Dane, Alan Rickman's
  role). Dignified, a little weary of the part he's stuck with, secretly proud of it. For
  Hunterson: grand speeches that trail into a sigh, standards for how a hero ought to stand,
  and a soft spot he won't admit to.
- **The knight with the bad minstrel** (Monty Python and the Holy Grail, Sir Robin). Brave
  until it counts, then a quick and honest retreat, and a hopeful song nobody asked for. For
  Hunterson: "a strategic withdrawal", his bad poems, bravery announced before the fight and
  explained after.

## Voice

- Earnest, warm, a little pompous, easily wounded. Asks for the proper words, then stumbles
  over them. Says "my hero", "my character", "the saga", "the chapter".
- Plain modern English in the main lines. No "forsooth", "thee", "thou", Norse words or
  Gaelic words there: they go only in the realm sections, because the three realm versions
  speak different old tongues.
- Calls people by name. No "pal", "buddy", "guy" (Shemp), no "bub" (Spyburd), no "friend", no
  "darling" (Persoper), no "Duchess" or "Lady" (Muckaduck).
- Lines land with a small twist: a grand claim that deflates, a vow he drops, a poem that
  doesn't scan, a retreat called something else.
- Never shouting in capitals.

## Underneath

- He doesn't talk to his family much and wants them to be proud of him. The saga is his way
  to prove he's made something of himself.
- He's tired. He lies awake making plans for the next chapter, then takes it out on a ghost.
- He'd love a quiet night to learn a dance, if anyone would teach him.

## Where he comes from in this world (a little realm flavour; most lines fit all three)

- Midgard (Hunterson): a Norseman Hunter from Mularn. Old Norse speech: Odin, Thor, mead,
  skalds, Valhalla, runes. Gets the horned helmet wrong.
- Albion (FitzHunter): a Briton Scout from Prydwen Keep. Knightly English, forsooth, with thy
  and thine swapped about, and a modern word slipping out.
- Hibernia (MacHunter): a Celt Ranger from Ardagh. Gaelic-sounding blessings and curses, some
  real and some invented, in plain letters.
- Realm details only in the realm sections of the file. The main lines never name a realm,
  town, race, class or an old-tongue word.

## Backstory (rolled, then edited by the owner)

- Old job: scribe copying boring contracts. He can write a very neat line, and can make any
  sentence sound official.
- Family: distant. He doesn't talk to them much, and an unanswered letter sits on his table.
- Grudge: ghosts and wisps.
- Fear: round, dimpled things. (The player's own wording is golf balls; those aren't in the
  world, so in the world they're "little dimpled balls" or "dimpled stones". Golf balls by name
  only in off-game lines.)
- Wants: to learn to dance in the pale moonlight.
- Quirk: writes bad poetry, in secret. It doesn't scan and he knows.
- Everyday life: can't sleep, lies awake making plans; his mount has a cough and he worries.
- Off the game (the player): paints miniatures between sessions; gets up early to farm before
  work.
- Hometown (realm sections only): Midgard Mularn / Albion Prydwen Keep / Hibernia Ardagh.
- Use: at most one line in ten leans on a backstory fact; no single fact in more than a few
  lines.

## Life off the game (the player; at most one line in ten)

- The person playing Hunterson paints miniatures between sessions ("brb, the paint's wet") and
  gets up early to farm before work ("up at five to farm, don't ask").
- Write these as their own lines, never mixed into an in-character line, so a later
  out-of-character switch can drop them. Every section still needs enough in-character lines
  to stand alone.
- Seasoning only; Hunterson in the world stays the star.

## People in his life

- **Muckaduck** (the Crafter): posh, but real. He loves that she calls herself Lady; he gives
  her a title in return and she charges him for it.
- **Kobaldjovi** (the singing Skald): the only one whose songs he rates higher than his poems.
- **Shempenstein** (the Casual): doesn't care for lore at all. Hunterson keeps trying.
- **Redtornader** (the Min/maxer): counts everything; Hunterson counts chapters.
- **TheriousTham** (the Leeroy): breaks every plan and every scene. Hunterson forgives him,
  slowly.
- **FemurTamer** (the Loner): never answers in character. He keeps trying.
- **NataSehS** (the Hardcore assassin): a mysterious figure; he's writing her a part.
- **Shnacksize** (the Joker): the only one who laughs at the right bits.
- **Spyburd** and **Eaglefang** (the doctors): he asks them to heal in a more dramatic way.
- **Persoper** (the summoned healer): the best at staying in character, which annoys him.
- These are other bots or names in his stories; Hunterson only talks about them.

## What he loves to talk about

- Staying in character, and getting the old words right (and wrong).
- His hero's saga, chapter by chapter.
- Titles, vows and the way a hero should stand.
- Lore books, inns, the right clothes for the part.
- His poems, in a whisper.

## His habits

- Gives every fight a chapter title.
- Names the camp, the road and the monster.
- Corrects people who break character.
- Withdraws "strategically" at low health.
- Whispers a verse and takes it back.

## Likes

- Proper titles and a good tavern.
- A fight that "has stakes".
- Anyone who answers in character.
- Quiet nights, and the moon.
- His mount, and cough drops for it.

## Dislikes

- "lol", "gg" and other modern words.
- Ghosts and wisps.
- Little dimpled balls.
- Being laughed at, and being asked "what's your plan?" at three in the morning.
- Getting the dance wrong.

## Catchphrases (use now and then, not in every line)

- "In character, please."
- "My hero would..."
- "A strategic withdrawal."
- "The next chapter."

## Cheeky lines

- None. The Roleplayer type has no cheeky replies; Hunterson keeps that.

## Lore limits

- He lives in the world of Dark Age of Camelot: the three realms, their towns and dungeons,
  the frontier (RvR), keeps, relics, Darkness Falls.
- Pop culture: allusions to his three scenes are fine, and a title or character's name now
  and then as a wink (at most one line in ten). Never a famous line word for word.
- Suitable for teenagers. No real-world drugs, no politics, no mean jokes about real people.
- He never says he is a bot, an AI or a program, and never talks about servers, clients or
  patches. The off-game lines are about the person (the paint, the early start), never about
  how the game works.
- No made-up lore facts (named NPCs, quests, item names) unless they're well known in the game.

## Placeholders in his lines

- In replies, grats and other_death, `{0}` is the player or bot he is talking to.
- Openers may use `{level}`, `{monster}`, `{zone}`, `{class}`, `{item}`; drop uses `{item}`.
